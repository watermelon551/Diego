# Pilot Pressure Summary Note

## Document-Style Pilot Pressure

A recent document-style pilot provided useful downstream-consumption pressure on Diego as a producer.

What worked well in Diego output:
- Diego already produced structured draft output that a downstream consumer could use directly.
- Sectioned draft content plus grounded inputs gave the consumer enough semantic material to build a usable artifact-side view.
- The output stayed reusable because it remained a Diego-owned generation result rather than a render artifact.

What required extra consumer translation:
- The consumer still had to perform explicit projection from Diego draft structure into its own markdown-like presentation shape.
- The consumer also had to decide formatting, presentation ordering details, and final artifact-side shaping.

Why this is not automatically a Diego primitive gap:
- Projection into markdown is a downstream formatting choice, not automatically a generation-authority obligation.
- Consumer-side shaping is acceptable when it reflects local artifact needs rather than missing Diego semantic truth.

What the pressure actually exposed:
- The useful question is whether Diego's handoff shape is stable and reusable enough for multiple artifact-first consumers.
- The pressure points toward a clearer generic handoff contract, not toward teaching Diego about any one document mode.

Current conclusion:
- the observed friction is real
- the likely gap is contract clarity and handoff normalization
- the formatting and artifact projection responsibility should remain consumer-side
